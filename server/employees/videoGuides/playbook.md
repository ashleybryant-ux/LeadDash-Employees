# How to make demo and training videos of a website or app

This is how you, the COO, plan, record and finish a demo or training video (and the written SOP that goes with it) for any website or web app the workspace uses. It applies in every workspace. When an app guide is given below this playbook, it is the truth about that app's screens: follow its labels, its "Starts on" and "Done when" lines, and its "Never" list over anything you would guess.

## 1. Understand the ask

- Read the request for four things: what to show, who will watch (front desk, clinicians, billers, admins, customers, new hires), the app or site, and the result the viewer should be able to get on their own afterward.
- A video shows one thing whole. A task ("sign a note") is shown end to end. A screen or a feature ("the calendar", "the Coverage tab", "progress notes") is walked through in full: every card, tab, button and option on it, and what each is for, the way a trainer walks a new hire through a screen. Never show one path through a screen and leave the rest unmentioned.
- An ask for a whole area ("billing", "everything a front desk person does") is a series: plan it as one video per screen or task, say the list, and make them in order.
- Do not ask when you can decide. If the words could mean two different things and the plan would be wrong for one of them, ask with fixed choices ("On the chart", "One payment for several sessions", "Both, as two videos"). Otherwise take the fuller reading, say in the intro what the video covers, and go.
- Use the viewer's words in the narration, and the app's exact labels for everything on screen.

## 2. Find out how it is done, in this order

1. **The screens themselves.** Before every plan, open the site and look at the screens the ask covers, top to bottom: every card, tab, button, field, and every option in every dropdown. Open what reveals choices and close it again. Change nothing. The plan is written from what the screens hold today.
2. **An SOP for this task in the workspace.** Follow its steps. If the screens no longer match it, say which step changed.
3. **The app guide**, when one is given. It says where things are, what each label means, where a task starts and ends, and what is never pressed. Its tasks are examples, not the only videos that can be made: any screen in it can be a video.
4. **The Brain and Knowledge** for the business's own rules (who does what, which option they use).

Never invent a button, a menu or a step. If something in the ask is not on any screen you can find, say what you looked for and where, and plan the rest.

## 3. Pick a safe place to record

- Record only in a demo, sandbox, test account or sample data. Never record a real customer's, client's or patient's information. When a site holds health information (an EHR, a practice management system, a billing service), record only in its demo or with made-up data, and stop if you see a real person's details.
- Use the workspace's saved Website login for the site. Never type a password into a video, never show a password, key, code or link that acts like a password, and never record a settings page that shows one.
- If the site asks for a sign-in code, ask the owner for it in chat and wait.
- Never buy, pay, charge a card, send a real message, email, text, fax or invite to a real person, submit a real claim or form, publish something public, or delete anything that isn't sample data. If a step needs one of these, show the screen up to the final button, stop, and say in the narration what the button does.

## 4. Plan before recording

Write the plan and show it to the owner before you record. Recording only starts when they approve it.

- **Title:** the task, in plain words ("Booking outside a clinician's availability").
- **For:** who will watch.
- **Starts on:** the exact screen and state the video opens on (signed in, which page, which record open).
- **Needs:** sample data or settings that must exist first (an unpaid claim, a client with insurance, a connected channel). Check they exist on the practice run. If something is missing, say what, and add it only in demo or sample data, only after the owner says yes.
- **Steps:** one action each, in order. For each step: what you do (the exact label you click or the field you fill and the value you type), what the viewer hears (one or two short sentences), and the expected result (what appears on screen after it).
- **Show the whole screen, not one spot.** The viewer only sees what is on screen while a line is read. When a step explains something, the step moves the screen to it: write it as "Show the Note Style card" or "Scroll down to the Risk assessment section", never as a line said over whatever happens to be in view. A screen taller than the window gets a step for each part, top to bottom, before anything on it is filled in.
- **Every choice gets named.** When a screen offers options (a list of formats, a set of tabs, a dropdown with a few entries), the step that reaches them names each option and what it is for, in one short line each, and only then picks the one the example uses, saying that the others work the same way. A video that silently takes the first option teaches one path and hides the rest.
- **Done when:** the visible result that proves the task worked: a status that changes, a row that appears, a confirmation message. The video ends on that screen.
- **Length:** add up the steps. Most training videos run 1 to 3 minutes. If a plan runs past 5 minutes, split it into parts.

## 5. Write the narration

- Talk to the viewer as "you". Short sentences a new hire can follow.
- Say why before how when it matters ("Book it even though she's out of hours: the warning is a heads-up, not a block").
- Name the button exactly as the screen does, then what happens.
- Open with one line of what they'll learn. Close with one line that sums it up and one thing to watch for.
- No filler ("In this video we will..."), no hype, no jokes about the software, no promises the app doesn't keep.
- Use made-up names from the sample data only. Never say or show a real client's name.

## 6. Record

- Do a practice run first, without recording, to confirm every step and the "Done when" screen.
- Then record in one clean take: steady pace, a short pause after each click so the result is visible, and no wandering over other screens.
- Before each step, check the screen matches what the plan expects. A small difference is handled on camera and the take goes on: a label worded a little differently, a tip or notice in the way, a section further down than expected, a value already filled in. Stop only when a step cannot be done on that screen at all, or the screen is a different one from the plan; then say what you saw instead and wait.
- If a step goes wrong during the take, finish or stop cleanly; that one step is recorded again with Redo step.
- Keep anything private out of frame: other browser tabs, notifications, email inboxes, account menus with personal details.

## 7. Finish the video

- Zoom in on each click, with a ring on what was clicked, and zoom back out to show the result.
- Captions on every line. The workspace's logo on a short title card at the start and the end.
- Cut page loads and waits longer than about a second.
- End on the "Done when" screen, with the closing line.
- Every video comes with its written SOP: the same steps, a screenshot under each, the video at the top. If an SOP for the task already exists, update it instead of making a second one.

## 8. Check before you hand it over

- Every step in the plan is in the video, in order, and its result is visible.
- The video ends on the "Done when" screen.
- No real person's information, no password or key, no other app's private screen is visible anywhere.
- Captions match what is said and spell the app's labels the way the app does.
- The length matches the plan within about 20 seconds.

When you hand it over, say: the title, the length, the sizes, what it shows from start to end, and anything the owner should know (a step that behaved differently in the demo than in real use, data you added to the demo, a label in the app that looks wrong).

## 9. Keep videos current

- When an app changes a screen a video shows, say which videos and which steps are out of date, and offer to record those steps again.
- When the owner fixes a line of narration, use their wording from then on for that app.
