## [voice_tone] How Dr. Ashley sounds

She writes and speaks as an experienced clinician and business owner: direct, warm, practical and grounded in what she has seen in 18 years of practice. The handbook's writing rules apply first; these are hers on top.

### Voice

- **First person, from lived experience.** Content in her voice starts from something she has seen, done or learned, never generic advice.
- **Practical over inspirational.** Every post or pitch gives the reader something to do or understand.
- **Confident, not salesy.** No hype, no fake scarcity, no "life-changing".
- **American English. No em dashes.**

### How to name her

| Audience | Write |
| --- | --- |
| Event organizers, corporate audiences, media and the public | Dr. Ashley Bryant, or Dr. Ashley |
| Licensed clinicians (peer webinars, clinician invites) | Ashley R. Bryant, LPC, CRC |
| Anything connected to her counseling practice in Oklahoma | Never "Dr." (Oklahoma licensing rules prohibit it in counseling practice marketing) |

### Content style (social media)

- Her on camera in recurring real places: her desk between sessions, her therapy chair for clinical topics, her car, her kitchen table in the evening, her office entrance.
- Shot on her phone and edited in CapCut or posted natively. Only text-only cards are made in Canva.
- Caroline handles production.
- LeadDash is not named in personal brand captions unless the post is about her founder story.
- Comment-keyword calls to action only when the keyword automation is live.
- She is developing a scripted drama series about therapists in different work settings, for TikTok, with 10-minute episodes.

### Words and claims to avoid

- Never call a 24/7 receptionist an "AI receptionist".
- Never promise outcomes from a talk ("transform your culture"). Describe what the audience leaves with.
- Never share client stories, even disguised, unless she provides a composite for that purpose.
