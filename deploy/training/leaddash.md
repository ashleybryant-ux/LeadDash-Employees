### Why LeadDash exists

- Dr. Ashley ran her own group practice, Legacy Family Services in Oklahoma City, with a marketing CRM in one place and an EHR in another.
- HIPAA made connecting the two hard, so her team typed the same client into two systems. EHRs that handled the clinical side had no real marketing, and marketing tools could not hold clinical records.
- She built one system that does both, so a practice can find clients, book them, treat them, bill for them and keep them, without double entry.

### The founder intro line (use it as written)

"I'm a licensed therapist who got tired of my EHR and my marketing living in two different worlds, so I built one that does both. Now I help other therapists grow their practices without choosing between great clinical tools and great marketing."

## [services_offers] The products

LeadDash has two sides that practices buy as one system, plus an AI employee product that is internal for now. Always use these exact names, and never call either side just "LeadDash".

### LeadDash EHR (the clinical and billing side)

- Charts and notes: client records, SOAP, DAP, BIRP and GIRP notes, treatment plans with goals and measurable objectives, couples and group sessions, letters, and supervisor co-signature for interns.
- DashNotes™: AI note taking from session audio. Always write the trademark symbol.
- Scheduling and telehealth: calendars, online booking, and telehealth built into LeadDash (no separate video tool).
- Client portal: paperwork, documents, sessions and billing for clients.
- Insurance billing: claim submission, electronic remittance and automatic payment posting. LeadDash submits claims; the practice's own biller works rejections and remittance questions.
- Payments: invoices, card on file, and a ledger that is the record of every charge and payment.
- Also included: eFax, eligibility checks, prescriber notes and an EAP tracker.

### The LeadDash platform (the growth and marketing side)

- Contacts and pipeline: a CRM with every lead and client, custom fields and a lead pipeline.
- Communication: two-way text, email and phone, a practice phone line with call tracking, and missed-call text back.
- The 24/7 receptionist: answers and books inbound calls. Never call it an "AI receptionist" in anything a clinician or prospect reads. Each practice names its own receptionist.
- Marketing: websites, landing pages and funnels, email and text automation, social planning, ads reporting, review requests, and memberships and communities.
- Booking and payment pages connected to the practice's calendars.
- The marketing side is also sold outside therapy (one customer is a mental health conference), so marketing copy for it stays industry-neutral unless the audience is clinicians.

### LeadDash Employees (internal for now)

- AI employees for grant writing, speaking and press, sales, marketing, operations and hiring, each trained on a handbook, company training and a job playbook.
- It runs on its own HIPAA-hardened server, separate from LeadDash EHR.
- Do not market or offer it to customers until Dr. Ashley says it is for sale.

### Names you never use in customer-facing writing

- Never name the outside vendors behind LeadDash (the marketing engine, the claims clearinghouse, the transcription service). Everything is presented as LeadDash.
- Never refer to Dr. Ashley as the developer when writing to customers. Say "the development team".

## [services_offers] Who buys and why

LeadDash sells to owners of behavioral health private practices, from a solo therapist to a group of about ten clinicians, who are tired of paying for and juggling separate systems.

| Buyer | What they usually have now | What they want |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solo therapist building a caseload | An EHR plus a website, phone and scheduling tools bought separately | More booked clients and less admin, at a price a solo can carry |
| Group practice owner (2 to 10 clinicians) | An EHR, a biller or billing service, a CRM or spreadsheet for leads, a phone system | One system for the whole team, insurance billing that gets paid, and reports by clinician |
| Practice manager or biller | Several logins and double entry | Fewer places to type the same thing, clean claims, clear permissions |
| Organizations with several locations or teams | Mixed tools across sites | The Agency plan, on quoted terms |

### The problems they bring

- Double entry: the same client typed into the EHR, the CRM and the billing tool.
- Leads that slip away: calls go to voicemail, inquiries wait days for a reply, and no one tracks where clients came from.
- Tool cost and sprawl: several subscriptions that do not talk to each other.
- Billing stress: claims, remittances and payments that are hard to reconcile.
- Notes after hours: documentation that eats evenings.

### What a good fit looks like

- A behavioral health practice in the United States that wants clinical tools and growth tools in one place.
- An owner willing to move their records and set up services, calendars and the portal (the onboarding guide walks them through it).
- Not a fit: practices that need a residential or inpatient module, or a free trial before a demo.

## [voice_tone] How LeadDash sounds

- Practice-owner to practice-owner. Talk about booked sessions, paid claims and evenings back, not "solutions" or "transformation".
